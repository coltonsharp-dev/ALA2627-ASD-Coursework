Week 9 · 21–25 September

Make it interactive

This week your site starts doing things. But before you build anything, you have to be able to say what you want — and that turns out to be the hard part. Here is how today works. The eight questions at the top of this page are your bell work. Some you type, some you pick from a list, one is a slider — and none of them go in your notebook. As you answer them your choices appear inside the prompt further down this page. You answer once and the prompt builds itself. When they are all answered, scroll down to the prompt and press the Copy button on it. Do not highlight the text and copy it by hand — the button copies the finished version with your answers already in it, and hand-copying is how you end up sending the chat a prompt that still says ANSWER 2. Then the thing worth learning: you get back exactly what you asked for. Ask for "make my site better" and you get something generic that fits anybody. Ask for "a click event listener on my #next button that changes the heading text to the next item in my array" and you get the thing you actually wanted — because every one of those words told it something. That is why the vocabulary list matters this week. Those words are not there to be memorised for a quiz. They are the words that make you understood. The more of them you use correctly, the closer the answer lands to what is in your head. Last rule, and it does not change all year: it plans, you type. You never paste code you cannot explain. You will be asked. Your project has a console panel along the bottom of the page. School Chromebooks block the browser's own console, so that panel is where JavaScript talks to you — every console.log and every red error line shows up there.
