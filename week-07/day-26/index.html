Hover, focus and active states

Thursday, 10 September 2026

Today's objective

Give everything clickable on my page a visible hover, focus and active state.

Course learning outcome

I can turn a plain HTML page into something that looks designed — colour, type, spacing and layout.

You have met today's objective when

  • I can write a :hover rule that changes something visible
  • I can write a :focus rule and say who it is for
  • I can use :active for the moment the mouse is actually down
  • I can add a transition so the change is not a jump cut
  • I can tab through my whole page and always see where I am

Read this first

Short day, one idea, and it is the first time your CSS reacts to a person.

Here is the part that matters beyond today's grade. :hover only exists if you have a mouse. Plenty of people navigate a page with the Tab key — because they cannot use a mouse, or because their hands are full, or because it is faster. For them the equivalent of hovering is :focus. If you style :hover and skip :focus, your page does not look slightly worse for those people, it stops telling them where they are at all.

So the rule for the rest of this course: every time you write a :hover, you write a :focus. Tab through your page before you call it done. If you lose track of where you are, it is not finished.
