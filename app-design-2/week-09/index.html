Week 9 · 21–25 September

Your first backend

Before anything else today: the three questions at the top of this page are your bell work, and you type your answers into the boxes underneath them — not in a notebook, and not in the chat. As you type, your words appear inside the prompt further down this page, so you write them once. When all three boxes have gone green, press the Copy button on the prompt rather than highlighting it by hand — the button takes the finished version with your answers already in it. Your server already answers. This week it starts answering questions, and by Friday it answers badly-asked ones properly too. Today you add the route every real backend has. A health check does one job: something asks whether the server is up, and the server says yes in the fewest words possible. No page, no design, no data — a small piece of JSON and a 200. Every company you will ever work for has this route, and it is usually the first one written and the last one deleted. You have two ways to see what your server is doing, and neither of them is the browser console — school Chromebooks block it. The first is the terminal you started the server in. Every request that arrives prints a line there: the method, the path and the status code it answered with. GET /api/health with a 200 on the end means it worked. The same line with a 404 means your server looked for that path and had nothing at it. Read that line before you touch anything else; it usually names the problem. The second is a browser tab. Your Codespace forwards your server to a real web address — it is in the Ports panel, next to the terminal, and it ends in app.github.dev. Open it, put /api/health on the end, and your JSON is on the screen. That address starts out Private, which means it only opens for you. In the Ports panel, right-click your port and set Port Visibility to Public, or the link you turn in shows me a sign-in page instead of your work.
