App Design 1 · Week 7 · Unit 02 — Make it look good

Spacing and visual rhythm

Wednesday, 9 September 2026

Today's objective

Space the parts of my page on a scale, so the spacing looks decided instead of accidental.

Course learning outcome

I can turn a plain HTML page into something that looks designed — colour, type, spacing and layout.

You have met today's objective when

  • I can name the spacing scale I am using and stay on it
  • I can choose between padding, margin and gap and say why
  • I can look at a page and point at the spacing that breaks the scale
  • I can space a group so things that belong together sit closer
  • I can explain why consistent spacing is what makes a page look designed

Read this first

Yesterday you decided where things go. Today you decide how much room is between them, which is the part most people never think about and the part that does most of the work.

Two ideas and that is the whole lesson. First, pick a scale and stay on it — 4, 8, 16, 24, 32. Not 7, not 13, not 22. A page where every gap comes off one scale looks deliberate even when you cannot say why. Second, spacing carries meaning: things closer together read as belonging together. If your heading is the same distance from its own paragraph as it is from the next section, you have told the reader they are unrelated.

The three tools are not interchangeable. Padding is inside a box. Margin is outside it. Gap is between children of a flex container — and gap is almost always the right answer for the space between cards, because margin on the last card leaves a gap you have to go back and delete.
